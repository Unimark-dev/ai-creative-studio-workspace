# Creative direction

Start from the idea, because good lighting cannot rescue an image with nothing to say. Before prompting, you should be able to finish this sentence: "The viewer sees ___ and understands ___."

## Think through the brief

- **Objective and audience:** what should change for whom? Infer these from context, and ask only if the answer changes the work.
- **Message:** one thing. An ad with three messages communicates none.
- **Product truth:** what about this product can be shown instead of said? Material, scale, use, origin, the moment it matters?
- **Visual idea:** the single device that carries the message. "A bottle on marble" is a setting. "The fragrance's light falling across a dark room like a window at dawn" is an idea.

## Art-direct it

Decide each of these on purpose:

- **Composition and hierarchy:** where the eye lands first, second, third
- **Product position and scale**
- **Camera:** angle, distance, lens feel (a low 35mm is heroic, 85mm at eye level is intimate)
- **Light:** source, direction, quality, and what it reveals about the material
- **Background and depth:** what surrounds the subject, and what is in focus
- **Negative space:** where, how much, and which copy it will hold. Plan RTL copy for Arabic, as the reading direction changes the composition's balance.
- **Brand color:** how it enters the scene through objects, light or surface, rather than a tint over the image

## The brief

Share this with the user in a few lines before generating:

```
Idea: <one sentence>
Scene: <subject, setting, camera, light>
Layout: <product position, copy position>
Brand: <how the palette and tone appear>
```

## Avoiding the generic

Default AI ads tend to share a look: a centered product on a pedestal, floating particles, smoke, gold bokeh, water splashes without a reason. If an element does not serve the idea, remove it. Vary composition across a campaign, and ask whether the image could only belong to this brand. If it could belong to anyone, push the idea further.
